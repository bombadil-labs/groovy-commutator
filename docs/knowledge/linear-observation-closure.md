# Closure under a linear observation is decided by its kernel; larger kernels keep only the affine rules

For each of the eight linear elementary rules applied once as an observation at cadence one, a rule is closed exactly when it maps kernel cosets into kernel cosets. On rings 6 to 12: the injective observations (204, 170, 240) and the constant one (0) close every rule; rules 60 and 102 at every ring, and rule 90 at odd rings, whose kernel is the complement pair, close exactly the 32 constant-complement-response rules and reproduce the parity history depths for all 256 rules; rule 90 at even rings (kernel with the alternating patterns) and rule 150 at rings divisible by 3 (kernel with the period-3 patterns, without the all-ones vector) close exactly the 16 affine rules and nothing else. Under those richer kernels the refinement depth reaches 4 within ring 12 and changes with ring size for some rules, so the two-step parity bound is specific to the complement-pair kernel. All tables are complement-invariant, and reflection acts as expected. See the [linear observations note](../research/2026-09-11-linear-observations.md). Scope: the eight linear observations, rings 6 to 12; the exact closed sets under the richer kernels were predicted only as bounds.
