# A rule cannot always choose itself from its own Groovy field

For the two constant elementary CA rules, `E_0(s)=0^n` and `E_255(s)=1^n`,
the candidate Groovy fields are respectively all zero and all one on
**every** source configuration of **every** positive ring width. A policy
specified by the simultaneous equation `r=F(G_r(s))` can consequently
have no solution (choose the opposite rule) or two solutions (choose the
matching rule). It does not generally define an autonomous update.

A causal policy can read `G_B(s)` under a fixed, declared reference rule
before selecting a different rule, or use an already completed mixed-rule
residual with stored past rows and rule identities. These observations
have different meanings and acquisition costs. A bounded local selector
with finite memory compiles to a fixed CA on augmented tracks; its
organization claim still needs an independent target and fair baseline.
