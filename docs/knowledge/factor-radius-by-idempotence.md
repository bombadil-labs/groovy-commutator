# Under an idempotent observation the factor is the observation composed with the rule, so its radius is at most 2

For an observation `ψ` with `ψ ∘ ψ = ψ`, the image is the fixed-point set, every image word is its own preimage, and the factor of any closed rule `r` is `ψ ∘ F_r` restricted to the image, a radius-2 local map. Rules 200 and 4 are idempotent; 232, 32 and 22 are not. On rings 6 to 12: under 200 the sixteen residual rules of the tenth unit have factor radius exactly 2 and the other 26 closed rules radius at most 1, which explains the non-elementary factors reported there; under 4 all 33 closed rules have radius at most 1. Under the non-idempotent 232, 32 and 22 every closed rule present at all seven rings has radius at most 1, a census fact with no theorem behind it. The one cell above radius 2 is rule 223 at ring 6 under 22, closed at that ring only, whose factor needs radius 3, the whole ring; a factor radius equal to the ring size is the signature of a finite-ring closure. Radius 0 includes cellwise factors such as the identity's, not only collapses. Radius tables are reflection-invariant and complement-covariant to the conjugate observations 236 and 223. See the [factor-radius note](../research/2026-09-11-factor-radius.md). Scope: five observations, rings 6 to 12, radii up to 3, closed rules only.
