# How does operation order change future options?

The finite part is partly answered. Preparing B(A(S)) versus A(B(S)) can
change future outcome counts, outcome identities, and responses to action.
These distinctions survive independent replay in the
[completed enumeration](../research/2026-09-07-future-repertoire.md).

A reset/complement example loses an outcome at one continuation tick and
regains it at two, while action responses remain different. A Rule 4/30
example has two disjoint two-outcome repertoires. Noncommutation therefore
does not supply a complete account of subsequent possibilities.

The test assumes fixed external actions and finite budgets. It leaves open
how an organization maintains its own capabilities, which outcomes matter
for its continuation, and how it acquires different effective operations.
The [revisable-primitives plan](../research/2026-09-07-revisable-primitives.md)
addresses one part of that larger question.
