# A finite ring window can overstate an all-ring depth claim, and at depth two it does so under one observation of eight

Refinement depth at most two is now certified at every ring size `n ≥ 4` for all 256 rules under the observations 232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 102, 90, 150: the certified eventual periods of `D²_ψ(n)` are 1, 2, 2, 1, 12, 1, 4, 6 with least onsets 14, 13, 13, 13, 31, 4, 9, 7, and no pair was censored. Against that certificate the fifteenth unit's rings-3-to-14 depth-two gap is exactly right under seven of the eight observations (20, 4, 4, 12, 0, 6, 12 rules) and wrong under 22, where twelve rules — 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111, 120, 121, 124, 125 — have depth at most two on every ring 3 to 14 and then leave `D²_22(n)`, all of them first at ring 24, each with an explicit closed violating walk and a certificate of period 12. The all-ring gap under 22 is therefore 18 rules, not 30. The conclusion that periodic configurations do not decide depth two survives for exactly the seven observations other than parity, now at every ring rather than only up to 14, but one of its eight numbers was 40% too large. The observation whose window failed is the one with the longest certified period and the latest onset, and the same mechanism explains a second failed bet in the same unit: the depth-two period under 22 looked like 3 on rings 9 to 14 because 3 divides the true period 12. Also observed, exactly and post hoc over these eight observations: `D²_ψ^∞` equals precisely the set of rules whose depth-two pair graph has no violating three-edge walk with both ends in one strong component, so at depth two the sufficient pruning condition is also necessary here. See the [depth-two-certificate note](../research/2026-09-12-depth-two-certificate.md). Scope: eight observations, depth two only; full-shift depth is the fifteenth unit's and is not recomputed, so the gap rules' full-shift depth at least three rests on that unit; no ring certificate at depth three; the necessity of the pruning condition is an observation on what was computed, not a proof.
