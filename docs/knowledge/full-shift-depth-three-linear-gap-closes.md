# Whether periodic configurations decide a depth level is a property of the observation, not of the level alone

Under the self-dual linear observation 150, the rings-3-to-14 depth-three gap is empty: `FS³_150` (246 rules) equals the intersection of the exhaustive rings-3-to-14 depth-three sets exactly, so on this observation periodic configurations already decide full-shift depth three. Under the linear observation 90, the same gap is nonempty (rules 41, 97, 107, 121, each with depth at most three on every ring 3 to 14 and at least four on the line). Both observations had a nonempty depth-two gap in the fifteenth unit (6 rules under 90, 12 under 150); at depth three the gap survives under 90 and closes under 150. So the sixteenth unit's finding that the full-shift and ring ladders "separate at every level above closure" is not uniform across observations or depth levels: it is a property of the pair (observation, level). The correlation with the observation's kernel structure (90 alternates with ring parity; 150 is nontrivial only on multiples of 3) is noted but not established as a mechanism. See the [full-shift-depth-three-linear note](../research/2026-09-12-full-shift-depth-three-linear.md). Scope: two linear observations, full-shift depth at most three, rings 3 to 14; no ring certificate at depth two or three, so the empty gap under 150 is a rings-3-to-14 statement, not an all-ring theorem — a larger ring could still separate.
