# A retention–history discriminator fails fresh Class-IV separation

An independent finite ECA pilot combines endpoint-conditioned same-site retention, rotation-quotient cycle growth on rings 9–14, and held-out older-history predictive gain multiplied by residual local uncertainty. Its post-discovery composite separates 54/110 on the original samples, but fresh runs rank Class-II families 9 and 73 above 110. Both positives remain above every included Class-III family in those fresh runs.

Rule 110's history becomes more predictive while residual uncertainty shrinks. Their product falls. In the population entropy limit, multiplying gain by residual uncertainty rewards a balance between the two; it does not measure an automaton's counterfactual future capacity. This observation limits that proxy and does not refute an exchange-rate hypothesis about past retention and available futures.

Scope: 88 reflection/complement-conjugation families, two primary positive families, fixed finite rings and random initial-state ensembles. All original failures, fresh failures and disputed-label comparisons are retained. No universal class definition, asymptotic recurrence claim, or class relabeling follows.
