# History improves prediction around ether departures

## Current finding

In the frozen Rule 110 regional experiment, six previous observed times
reduce next-bit error in departure neighborhoods under every tested observer,
ring width, detector scale, training ensemble, and held-out random seed.
The tested regions are defined by local incompatibility with coherent ether
patches, expanded over each target's fine causal footprint.

At width 840 with the primary detector, departure error falls from 33.21% to
2.21% for parity blocks of two and from 41.06% to 7.74% for parity blocks of
four. Majority and derivative observations improve too. Departures contribute
40.4–74.8% of total gain across the tested widths, observers, and detectors.

## Scope and what remains open

This is repeated computational evidence with two training ensembles and eight
shared held-out trajectories, not independent external replication. Labels
do not identify particle species, collisions, or computational activity.
Background and ambiguous regions also improve. Departure neighborhoods still
contain 97.2–100% of remaining error in primary-detector pooled results.

The next question concerns those residual errors and whether their structure
can be identified independently. Full methods, seed ranges, controls, and
data are in [the regional experiment](../research/2026-09-07-ether-regions.md).
