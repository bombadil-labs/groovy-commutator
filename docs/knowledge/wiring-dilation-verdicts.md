# Dilating the neighborhood is a relabeling, a change, or a split, depending on the factor and on whether the observation travels

Reading an elementary rule table at offsets `{−d, 0, +d}` on the `n`-ring is a change of wiring with the rule table fixed. When `gcd(d, n) = 1` and the observation is dilated with the rule, the site permutation `i ↦ d·i mod n` conjugates the dilated automaton to the standard one, so closure, commutation, collapse, the residual class, refinement depth and the result-1 commutator classification are all exactly preserved; verified for `d ∈ {2, 3}`, rings 6 to 12, observations 232 and 4. When the observation is left standard, the same rewiring is a change: at rings 7, 9 and 11 with `d = 2` the closed set under majority shrinks from 22 to 12 rules and under isolated-cell detection from 33 to 17, the observation rule leaves the commuter set, and the depth tables move; the partial census equals the standard census under the observation rewired to the inverse offset `d^{−1} mod n`. When `d` divides `n`, the automaton is `d` interleaved copies of the `n/d`-ring and every census equals the small-ring census, including depth and the commutator classification, which departs from the result-1 sets only on components with fewer than 5 cells. See the [wiring-dilation note](../research/2026-09-11-wiring-dilation.md). Scope: dilation on a ring only; nothing about other offset sets, 2D lattices or block constructions.
