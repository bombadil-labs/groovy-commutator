# Transverse intervention independence survives release of total target storage

**Status: exact representation/resource theorem with bounded implementation replay.** The [intervention-axis protocol](../research/protocols/intervention-axis-20260911.md) and [result note](../research/2026-09-11-intervention-axis.md) turn the established separated-Rule90-strip action theorem into an operational axis witness.

For every finite number `m` of separated strips, the translated logical-bit actions give `m` independent generators of physical support two, so native local intervention rank is at least `m`. Under the copied-width control, a source-bit flip that remains in the copied family at edit time costs `2m` physical cells and no proper nonempty subset of channel flips is image-preserving.

A one-dimensional target may use arbitrarily much total storage as `m` grows. If its alphabet size `q` and one common endpoint window of `K+2R` sites are fixed, however, preserving all subsets of the `m` locally anchored source actions requires `2^m <= q^(K+2R)`. Thus sufficiently many independently addressable transverse channels cannot be hidden while keeping that local endpoint resource fixed. This is an intervention-local resource obstruction, not an intrinsic-dimension theorem.

The bounded verifier checks `m=1..6`, rings 5 and 7, three deterministic base families, every action subset and two coarse updates; all controls pass. The touching-strip control independently reproduces 17/64 valid versus 47/64 invalid local inputs, showing that instantaneous editability without dynamical closure is not enough.

Scope: one prepared separated-strip representation, supplied action interface, fixed common endpoint-window contract. No self-assembly, endogenous control, or claim that non-spatial registers cannot realize similar intervention structure.
