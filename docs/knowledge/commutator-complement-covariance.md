# The commutator is complement-covariant exactly for self-dual rules

Under complement conjugation `S ↦ ¬S`, `r ↦ r̃` with `r̃(l,m,r) = ¬r(¬l,¬m,¬r)`, the native derivative `D = 𝟙 ⊕ F` is a difference and transports unchanged, while the law acts on labeled states. The native commutator therefore satisfies `G_{r̃}(¬S) ⊕ G_r(S) = F_r(x) ⊕ ¬F_r(¬x)` at `x = D_r(S)`: it is covariant for every state iff `r` is self-dual, `r̃ = r`. Sufficiency is immediate; necessity is proved by the exhaustive local check, since the defect has radius 2 and all 32 five-cell source words decide it: exactly the 16 self-dual rules pass and every other rule has a witness (proof route recorded 2026-09-11). Rings 6, 8, 10 agree with no spurious passes. Transporting the derivative as a state, `D ↦ ¬D`, makes all 256 rules covariant at the cost of one complement of the derivative field.

Consequences for established result 1: the commutator of an affine rule is its bias, and the bias transforms as `c ↦ c ⊕ M𝟙 ⊕ 1`. Rule 0 maps to Rule 255 and `G ≡ 0` to `G ≡ 1`; the nonlinear zero-G rules 4 and 200 map to 223 and 236, whose commutators vary with the state. Reflection preserves the classification for all 256 rules.

The criterion is narrower than the one for factoring through the full gradient, which needs a constant complement response and therefore also admits the constants 0 and 255.
