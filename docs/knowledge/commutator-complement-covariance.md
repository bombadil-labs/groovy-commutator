# The commutator is complement-covariant exactly for self-dual rules

Under complement conjugation `S ↦ ¬S`, `r ↦ r̃` with `r̃(l,m,r) = ¬r(¬l,¬m,¬r)`, the native derivative `D = 𝟙 ⊕ F` is a difference and transports unchanged, while the law acts on labeled states. The native commutator therefore satisfies `G_{r̃}(¬S) ⊕ G_r(S) = F_r(x) ⊕ ¬F_r(¬x)` at `x = D_r(S)`: it is covariant for every state iff `r` is self-dual, `r̃ = r`. Exhaustively at rings 6, 8, 10 the covariant rules are exactly the 16 self-dual ECA, with no spurious passes. Transporting the derivative as a state, `D ↦ ¬D`, makes all 256 rules covariant at the cost of one complement of the derivative field.

Consequences for established result 1: the commutator of an affine rule is its bias, and the bias transforms as `c ↦ c ⊕ M𝟙 ⊕ 1`. Rule 0 maps to Rule 255 and `G ≡ 0` to `G ≡ 1`; the nonlinear zero-G rules 4 and 200 map to 223 and 236, whose commutators vary with the state. Reflection preserves the classification for all 256 rules.

The self-duality criterion is the same one that governs factoring through the full gradient. See the [audit note](../research/2026-09-11-representation-invariants-audit.md). Scope: elementary CA, two declared transformations; no claim about other relabelings.
