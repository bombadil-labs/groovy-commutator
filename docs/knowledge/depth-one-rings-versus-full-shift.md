# Depth at most one is certified at every ring, and the periodic configurations do not exhaust full-shift depth

For an observation `ψ` and rule `r`, pairs agreeing on `ψ` and on `ψ ∘ F_r` are the closed walks in a 256-vertex pair graph; a seven-cell pair block whose two-step observed successors disagree is a three-edge walk, and `h_* ≤ 1` fails at ring `n ≥ 4` exactly when such a walk has a return walk of length `n − 3`. Boolean powers of the graph's matrix repeat within 33 steps for every one of the 2,048 pairs, so the depth-one set at every ring is decided finitely. Under 232, 4, 32 and 200 the depth-one sets are constant from rings 9, 9, 9 and 10 (118, 81, 81, 144 rules); under 22 the sequence has period 3 in the ring but settles only at ring 28 (183 rules off multiples of 3, 115 on them); under parity 102 it is 248 rules from ring 5; under 90 and 150 it alternates forever with periods 4 and 6, far above the 16 affine rules that closure gives on those rings. Full-shift depth one implies depth one at every ring, but the converse fails under 232, 200, 22 and 102 (16, 8, 22 and 8 rules): a violating walk can be bi-infinitely extendable while lying on no closed walk. Closure had no such gap in the thirteenth unit; depth does, so a depth claim must say whether it concerns rings or the full shift. See the [depth-one-certificate note](../research/2026-09-11-depth-one-certificate.md). Scope: eight observations, depth at most one only; depth two is not certified.
