## The question

Every closure census in this program had been a ring-by-ring enumeration on rings 6 to 12, and two of them were ring-dependent: rule 223 under observation 22 closes at ring 6 but at none of rings 7 to 12, and the linear observations 90 and 150 alternate with the parity of the ring or its divisibility by 3. Nothing said what happens at ring 13, or 40. This unit replaces the census by a certificate. For an observation `ψ`, the pairs `(x, y)` of ring configurations with `ψ(x) = ψ(y)` are exactly the closed walks of length `n` in a sixteen-vertex **pair graph** `G_ψ` (vertices: two-cell pair blocks; edges: three-cell pair blocks on which `ψ` agrees). A rule `r` fails closure at ring `n` when some five-cell pair block whose observed successors disagree at the centre, a **violating three-edge walk** `v_0 → v_1 → v_2 → v_3`, lies on a closed walk of length `n`; for `n ≥ 4` that is a return walk of length `n − 3` from `v_3` to `v_0`, read off the boolean power `A_ψ^{n−3}`. Boolean powers of a sixteen-by-sixteen matrix repeat, `A^{k+p} = A^k`, so the closed sets are periodic in `n` from `k + 3` with period `p`, and the finite list `C_ψ(4), …, C_ψ(k+p+2)` is the whole all-ring census.

## Answer

- **K1, divisibility (theorem control).** A closure lost at ring `n` is lost at every multiple: `C_ψ(kn) ⊆ C_ψ(n)` held for every pair of exhaustive rings 3 to 14 and all eight observations.
- **K2, the criterion is exact (theorem control).** The graph criterion reproduces the exhaustive closed sets at every ring 4 to 14 for all eight observations, and the exhaustive sets at rings 6 to 12 equal the twelfth unit's (232, 4, 32, 200, 22) and seventh unit's (102, 90, 150) recorded sets.
- **K3, the certificate (reported).** The matrix powers repeat early: `(k, p)` is (5, 1) for 232, (3, 1) for 4 and 32, (4, 1) for 200, (7, 1) for 22, (2, 1) for 102, (2, 2) for 90 and (2, 3) for 150. The certified lists therefore run to ring 8, 6, 6, 7, 10, 5, 6 and 7 respectively and decide every ring `n ≥ 4`. The eventual period of the closed sets is 1 for the first six observations, 2 for 90 and 3 for 150. Sizes of the all-ring closed sets: 22, 33, 33, 42, 11, 32, 16, 16.
- **K4, constant from ring 7 (bet, held).** For 232, 4, 32, 200, 22 and 102 the closed set at every ring `n ≥ 7` equals the all-ring closed set; no rule closes at any ring `n ≥ 7` without closing at all rings. Rule 223 under 22 is closed at rings 3, 4, 5 and 6 and at no ring `n ≥ 7`: ring 6 was the only exception inside the earlier 6–12 census, and the small rings below it also carry this closure. The small rings do carry extra closures: at ring 3 the closed sets have 64, 160, 160, 80, 160, 64 rules; at ring 4, 64, 104, 104, 80, 96, 32; at ring 5, 64, 37, 37, 42, 80, 32; at ring 6 every observation but 22 is already at its all-ring value. Under 4 the ring-5 extras are `{203, 217, 235, 249}`, under 32 `{40, 44, 96, 100}`.
- **K5, the linear observations (theorem parts and bets, all held).** The affine rules close under 102, 90 and 150 at every certified ring; 150 closes all 256 rules at every ring not divisible by 3, and exactly the 16 affine rules at every ring divisible by 3 from 6 on (bet e); 90 closes exactly the 32 constant-complement-response rules at every odd ring from 5 on and exactly the 16 affine rules at every even ring from 6 on (theorem part c and bet d). Reported: 90 closes 64 rules at ring 4, 150 closes 64 at ring 3.
- **K6, symmetries (theorem controls).** Complement covariance `C_{ψ̃}(n) = {r̃ : r ∈ C_ψ(n)}` and reflection covariance held at every ring 4 to 14 for all eight observations, the conjugate and mirrored observations' sets computed by the same criterion.
- **K7, all-ring closure equals full-shift closure (bet, held).** For all eight observations, a rule closed at every ring `n ≥ 4` is closed on the full shift and conversely: no violating walk is bi-infinitely extendable without lying on a closed walk. Under 4, 32, 22, 90 and 150 every vertex of the pair graph lies on a cycle; under 232, 200 and 102 some vertices (2, 6 and 8 of them) lie on no cycle, and the equality still holds.

Post hoc, labeled: observation 32 is observation 4 applied to the complemented state (`32(a, b, c) = 4(¬a, ¬b, ¬c)`), so `C_32(n) = {r̃ : r ∈ C_4(n)}` at every ring; the result file confirms this at rings 3 to 14, which is why the two censuses have identical size profiles.

## Reading for the program

For the six observations 232, 4, 32, 200, 22 and 102, the ring-by-ring censuses of units five to twelve were, for closure, complete after ring 6: each closed set they reported at rings 7 to 12 is the closed set at every ring from 7 on, and ring 6 differs only by rule 223 under 22. That is now a certified fact rather than an observation, and the certificate is cheap (sixteen-by-sixteen boolean powers, at most seven of them here). For the linear observations 90 and 150 the same census showed an alternation on rings 6 to 12, and the certificate now shows it never settles at any ring: their periods 2 and 3 are the periods of their kernels. Small rings (3 to 5) are a different regime, with dozens of closures that exist only because the whole ring fits inside the five-cell window of the criterion; the twelfth unit's warning sign, a factor that needs the whole configuration, is what those closures look like from the factor's side. For the primitives program, closure under an observation can be decided once, for all ring sizes, from the pair graph, so "closed at the sizes we tried" is no longer the best available statement.

## Limits

Eight observations, exhaustive rings 3 to 14, certified rings `n ≥ 4`. The certificate decides closure only; refinement depth `h_*` and factor radius are not certified beyond the rings where earlier units computed them. Nothing is claimed about observations outside the eight, and the constancy from ring 7 is a fact about these six, not a general one, as 90 and 150 show. Rings 1 and 2 are not treated. No Class IV, novelty, renormalization or dimension claim.
