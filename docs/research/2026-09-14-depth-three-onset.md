## The question

The [nineteenth unit](2026-09-13-closed-violation-depth-three.md) decided the all-ring depth-three set, the first failing ring `n_min` of every deep rule, and the eventual period `P³_ψ` — everything Lemma A's strong-component criterion gives directly. What it explicitly could not reach was a full **ring certificate**: the onset `N³_ψ` beyond which the eventual pattern is known to hold at every ring, not just eventually. The eighteenth unit had solved the analogous problem at depth two using sparse packed boolean powers of its 4096-vertex pair graph; the nineteenth unit's own checkpoint declared the same move "out of reach" at depth three on cost grounds — a power of the full 65,536-vertex matrix is `2^32` bits (512 MiB) and costs 20.8 seconds, and at roughly 800 pairs with potentially dozens of powers each, that is days.

A cheap feasibility probe, run before freezing anything, found the actual lever: the ring criterion only ever reads `(A^m)[v_3, v_0]` for a closed violating walk, and every walk between two vertices of one strong component stays inside it. So the power sequence never needs the full matrix — it can be taken on `A_U`, the adjacency restricted to the union of carrying components (the components containing a closed-violation endpoint). The nineteenth unit's own record shows those components have median 1,726 vertices and a recorded maximum of 14,168; a power there costs 64 milliseconds, not 20.8 seconds. That restriction lemma, not a bigger machine, is this unit's actual contribution — the eighteenth unit's onset machinery (`rule_period`, `rule_onset`, the `(k, p)` repeat-detection loop) lifts to depth three unchanged once it runs on `A_U` instead of the full matrix.

The probe also found the onset is not a formality: rules 94 and 133 under observation 232 — the nineteenth unit's two non-anchored, eventually-out rules under that observation — showed a rule onset of 65 on a scratch sample, more than double the depth-two record of 31. That became the protocol's headline bet.

## Reading for the program

Bet (b)'s failure is informative in the same way the program's other failed bets have been: the ceiling was set from the depth-two record (`N²_ψ ≤ 31` at most), and the actual depth-three onsets under 4 and 32 (22, sharing a conjugate pair of driving rules) simply exceed the naive ceiling derived that way — a reminder, consistent with the nineteenth unit's own finding, that ring windows and onsets are properties of the pair (observation, depth level), not values that carry up predictably from one level to the next.

The restriction lemma is the reusable result here. It converts "decide a ring property at depth `h`" from an object with cost governed by the *full* pair-graph size into one governed by the size of the *carrying components* — which can be, and empirically are, far smaller. The same move should generalize to any future ring-certificate question phrased in terms of closed violating walks, but each new domain still needs its own cost argument; this is not authorization for another depth census.
