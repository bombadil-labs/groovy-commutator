## The question

The [fifteenth unit](2026-09-11-full-shift-depth-two.md) found rules with refinement depth at most two on every ring from 3 to 14 but at least three on the full shift — 20, 4, 4, 12, 30, 0, 6, 12 rules under the observations 232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 102, 90, 150 — and could not say whether those rules keep depth two at *every* ring, because no ring certificate existed at depth two and its ring sets had not visibly settled by ring 14 (`|D²_232(12..14)| = 198, 200, 198`; `|D²_4(12..14)| = 105, 102, 101`). The [seventeenth unit's checkpoint](checkpoints/representation-invariants.md) left "a depth-two ring certificate if a cheaper matrix method is found" as a candidate. The method is the cheaper one: the fifteenth unit's depth-two pair graph `G²_{ψ,r}` has out-degree at most four, so a boolean power `A^m[u, ·] = OR_{w ∈ out(u)} A^{m−1}[w, ·]` is four row gathers and three ORs on rows of 64 packed 64-bit words, not a dense `4096³` product — measured at about 1.7 ms per power against 0.45 s for one dense `4096 × 4096` float matmul. The [fourteenth unit's](2026-09-11-depth-one-certificate.md) ring criterion then decides every ring at once: for `n ≥ 4`, `r ∉ D²_ψ(n)` iff some violating three-edge walk `(v_0, v_3)` whose ends lie in one strong component has `(A^{n−3})[v_3, v_0] = 1`, and since the power sequence is deterministic it eventually repeats, so a byte-confirmed certificate `(k, p)` decides the whole sequence. The questions: is every pair certified within the frozen cap of 1024 powers; what are the eventual periods `P_ψ` and onsets `N_ψ` of `D²_ψ(n)`; and is the all-ring gap `Γ²_{ψ,∞} = D²_ψ^∞ \ FS²_ψ` the same as the fifteenth unit's rings-3-to-14 gap.

Nothing about full-shift depth is recomputed here: `FS¹_ψ`, `FS²_ψ`, the rings-3-to-14 sets and the ring-14 gap are read from the fifteenth unit's result file with its hash. The graph itself is imported from that unit's script rather than restated.

## Answer

- **P1, criterion exact: held, in every part.** For all eight observations and every ring `4 ≤ n ≤ 14` the set decided by the criterion equals the fifteenth unit's recorded `D²_ψ(n)` — 88 ring-by-ring agreements, no exception. The pruning theorem held as a computed control: of the 102, 81, 81, 136, 93, 240, 130, 98 rules in `FS¹_ψ`, **not one** has a violating walk with both ends in a common strong component, so every depth-one rule is trivially certified at every ring with no power taken. `FS²_ψ ⊆ D²_ψ(n)` at every certified ring and the divisibility `D²_ψ(kn) ⊆ D²_ψ(n)` held with zero violations. The independent dense cross-check passed on all 21 frozen sample pairs (the three smallest rules with a closed violation per observation — 102 contributes none, all 256 of its rules being trivially certified): `A¹` through `A⁴⁰` agree byte for byte with an explicit dense `4096 × 4096` BLAS float32 reference taken in the opposite association, honouring gate-1 note (a).
- **P2, certificates: no pair censored anywhere.** Every one of the 256 rules under each of the eight observations, and under all seven complement-conjugate and mirror images as well (15 observations in total), is either trivially certified or carries a byte-confirmed `(k, p)`. Status counts (trivially certified / certified / censored): 232 `198/58/0`, 4 `101/155/0`, 32 `101/155/0`, 200 `184/72/0`, 22 `204/52/0`, 102 `256/0/0`, 90 `242/14/0`, 150 `234/22/0`. Largest preperiod `max_r k_r` = 26, 23, 23, 22, **40**, 0, 14, 15 (the record is rule 123 under 22, with `(k, p) = (40, 12)`). `L_ψ`, the lcm of the rules' periods: 12, 60, 60, 4, 60, 1, 24, 6. Per-rule periods are small: `p ∈ {1, 3, 4, 12}` under 232, `{1, 3, 4, 5, 12}` under 4 and 32, `{1, 2, 4}` under 200, `{3, 6, 12, 30}` under 22, `{4, 8, 12}` under 90, `{3, 6}` under 150. 15,852 powers were taken over the eight observations (1998, 4671, 4671, 1777, 1873, 0, 320, 542), and **zero hash collisions** occurred, so no certificate needed the delay path. The cap of 1024 was never approached.
- **P3, eventual periods: (c) and (d) held, (a) and (b) failed.** The least eventual periods `P_ψ` are **1, 2, 2, 1, 12, 1, 4, 6** and the least onsets `N_ψ` are **14, 13, 13, 13, 31, 4, 9, 7**.
  - (a) **failed.** `P_232 = 1` and `P_200 = 1` as bet, but `P_4 = P_32 = 2`: the sequence does not become constant under 4 or 32. In each case exactly one rule oscillates — **rule 50 under 4, rule 179 under 32** (its complement conjugate) — whose membership alternates with the ring parity from the onset; every other rule is eventually constant, so the bet fails on a single rule per observation.
  - (b) **failed.** `P_22 = 12`, not 3. The depth-one period under 22 was 3 and the recorded depth-two sizes at rings 9 to 14 looked like a mod-3 pattern, but the certified sequence has period 12 with onset 31: `|D²_22(n)|` runs 221, 238, 240, 222, 237, … at rings 12 to 16 and 237, 206, 237 at rings 23 to 25. Period 3 divides 12, so the earlier appearance was a sub-pattern of the true period, not the period.
  - (c) **held**, with zero exceptions: `D²_102(n)` is all 256 rules at every certified ring, `D²_90(n)` at every certified odd ring, and `D²_150(n)` at every certified ring with `3 ∤ n`.
  - (d) **held.** `P_90 = 4` (a multiple of 4) and `P_150 = 6` (a multiple of 6) — in fact exactly the fourteenth unit's depth-one periods under those two observations, 4 and 6.
- **P4, the all-ring depth-two gap: the unit's main bet failed, under 22 alone.** For seven of the eight observations `Γ²_{ψ,∞} = Γ²_{ψ,[3,14]}` exactly: 232 the same 20 rules, 4 the same 4 (9, 25, 65, 67), 32 the same 4 (61, 103, 111, 125), 200 the same 12, 102 empty as the theorem requires, 90 the same 6 (41, 57, 97, 99, 107, 121), 150 the same 12. Their certificates all have `p = 12` with `k` from 12 to 25, so their loss at 24 is a consequence of the period the fifteenth unit's ring window could not see. The headline survives in weaker form and with one number changed: the all-ring depth-two gap is nonempty for exactly the seven observations other than parity, of sizes **20, 4, 4, 12, 18, 6, 12**, so periodic configurations do not decide depth two at any ring, not only up to 14 — but under one of the eight the rings-3-to-14 window overstated the gap by 40%.
- **New, and not predicted: the trivial certificate is exactly the all-ring set.** Under every one of the eight observations, `D²_ψ^∞` equals precisely the set of rules with `V²_{cl} = ∅` — 198, 101, 101, 184, 204, 256, 242, 234 rules. So a rule has depth at most two at every ring iff its depth-two pair graph has no violating three-edge walk with both ends in a single strong component: the pruning lemma's sufficient condition turns out to be necessary as well, and every rule that has a closed violation anywhere loses depth two at some ring within `N_ψ + P_ψ − 1`. This is an exact post-hoc observation over these eight observations and all 256 rules, not a frozen prediction and not a proof for other observations.
- **P5, symmetries: held exactly.** Under complement conjugation and under reflection there are zero ring-set mismatches and zero certificate mismatches — status and `(k, p)` both — for all eight observations, and the graph correspondence itself passes for all 2,048 `(ψ, r)` pairs: the explicit vertex maps `v ↦ v ⊕ 4095` and cell reversal carry edge sets and violating-walk sets onto those of the conjugated and mirrored pairs, honouring gate-1 note (b). The conjugate and mirror observations are uncensored with the transported periods and onsets (60: `P=1, N=4`; 151: `12, 31`; 153: `1, 4`; 165: `4, 9`; 223: `2, 13`; 236: `1, 13`; 251: `2, 13`).

## Reading for the program

The fourteenth unit certified depth one at every ring and the fifteenth could not follow it to depth two. That obstacle was an implementation cost, not a mathematical one: with out-degree four and packed rows, depth two is certified at every ring for every rule under every one of the eight audited observations, with nothing censored and no certificate near the cap. The program can now state ring-side depth claims at level two without a ring bound attached.

What the failures say is about ring windows, not about depth. The fifteenth unit's rings 3 to 14 were enough to get the gap right under seven observations and wrong under one, and the way it was wrong is the ordinary way: the observation with the longest period (12) and the latest onset (31) is the one whose window was too short, and twelve rules that looked all-ring at ring 14 leave at ring 24. Symmetrically, P3(b)'s failure is a period-3 pattern that was really a divisor of a period-12 pattern. The practical lesson for this program's verdict matrix is that a census computed over an arbitrary finite ring range should be labelled with the certified onset and period of the quantity it measures, or else with the fact that they are unknown; "constant over the rings we computed" is a weaker claim than it reads, and under 22 it was false. The preceding units' ring censuses stop well before `N_22 = 31`, and nothing here revises them — they were each certified at the ring sizes they claim — but it does say which of them had the least room.

The unpredicted identity `D²_ψ^∞ = {r : V²_{cl} = ∅}` is the most suggestive thing in the run and the least established. It says that at depth two, over these observations, there is no rule whose closed violations all need a ring longer than the certified sequence — the dichotomy is between "no closed violating walk at all" and "fails at some ring". It does not say why, it is not proved for any observation outside the eight, and it is consistent with an accident of these particular graphs. It is the first concrete object the checkpoint's "structural account of the deep rules" candidate has to bet on, and it suggests the account should be sought in the strong-component structure of the pair graph rather than in the rule tables.

## Limits

Mirroring the protocol's Section 4. Nothing about depth three or beyond at any ring: a rule outside `D²_ψ^∞` has `h_* ≥ 3` on some ring and nothing more is certified about it, and no ring certificate at depth three is given or implied — that would need 65,536-vertex powers at 128 MB each. Nothing about full-shift depth: `FS¹_ψ` and `FS²_ψ` are the fifteenth unit's, read with their hash and not recomputed, so this unit certifies the ring side of the gap only and its twelve rules under 22 remain full-shift-depth-at-least-three by that unit's evidence, not this one's. Nothing about observations outside the eight, about rings 1 and 2, or about ring 3, which is reported from the fifteenth unit's exhaustive set (all 256 rules under every observation) and is not covered by the criterion. The cap of 1024 is a verifier constant, not a claim about any graph's index; it happens that nothing came near it, which makes the censoring contract untested in this run rather than vindicated. The identity between `D²_ψ^∞` and the trivially certified set is exact on what was computed and post hoc. No structural account of the gap rules is offered: the certificate says which rules have all-ring depth two and full-shift depth at least three, not why. No Class IV, universality, novelty, renormalization, dimension, physical or metaphysical claim.

Determinism was checked by full rerun, not by hashes alone: the canonical verifier was executed a second time from the same pinned tree and its output is byte-identical to the committed file. The run is off GitHub Actions by design under the 2026-09-12 CI-cost boundary; automatic CI on this unit carries only the seconds-scale integrity tier and the verifier's sub-two-minute `--self-test`, and the full byte-for-byte replay is `workflow_dispatch`-only behind an authorization input.
