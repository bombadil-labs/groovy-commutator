# Recursive Groovy: three different questions

2026-09-22. Authored by Codex after two independent, read-only Sol audits of
the active proof, research and knowledge records at main
`8d10669a5a2d5a13f34af341ecde2ec9bb1e6852`. Evidence: exact synthesis of
proved identities and preserved finite results. This note introduces no new
experiment and no new impossibility theorem.

## Question and decision

Why does the repository appear to say both that recursive Groovy transport is
proved and that recursive G remains open?

The statements concern different objects. **Ancestral source G is already
transported exactly through every finite lift depth by a two-beam
construction. Native descendant cellwise G is generally not determined by
the marked beam. Compressing the two-beam carrier into one marked point beam
plus a locally defined vector sector remains open.** The older period-four
P/D carrier census is a fourth, narrower finite construction and has no
all-depth proof.

These conclusions remove an ambiguity; they do not justify a new experiment.
The [consumer audit](2026-09-22-phase-free-consumer-audit.md) found no current
downstream operation needing the open one-beam interface, so both that
compression problem and further native-G carrier searches remain dormant.

## The objects that must not be conflated

### 1. Source G and the horizon commutators

For a source law `H`, let `D_H(X)=X XOR H(X)` and

```text
G_H(X) = D_H(H(X)) XOR H(D_H(X)).
```

More generally,

```text
K_t(X) = D_H(H^t(X)) XOR H^t(D_H(X)),   K_1 = G_H.
```

The product lift applies the proved point lift to both `X` and `D_H(X)`.
These are ordinary states on two copies of the same descendant beam. Decoding
the two evolved states recovers `K_t` at every finite lift depth, without
consulting an off-beam completion. This is an exact all-depth transport theorem
for an **ancestral source observable**.

`K_t` is not the repository's commutator-history sequence `G_H(H^t(X))`.
It accumulates and tangent-transports prior discrepancy according to the
cocycle in the [affine lift theorem](2026-09-17-affine-oriented-lift-theorem.md).

### 2. What the affine jet exposes

One marked affine jet stores the temporal differences
`D=X XOR H(X)` and `T=X XOR H^2(X)`. Comparing its secant update with the
separately typed vector/point encoding gives residual coordinates
`(0,0,K_1,K_2,0,0)`. Thus the jet makes Groovy structure locally available as
a **residual readout**. It does not make native descendant G literally one of
the six rows by itself.

For Rule 110, `G=D XOR T XOR H(D)` gives a four-bit marked readout. The
[cost comparison](2026-09-22-groovy-readout-cost.md) proves that a named
three-track cache supplies the same expression. That result is a readout
identity, not a phase-free typed Groovy bundle.

### 3. Native descendant cellwise G

Choose a total ambient completion `F` of a descendant partial rule and compute
its ordinary cellwise-XOR commutator `G_F`. The queried difference
configuration can leave the marked beam. Whenever a required local key is not
forced by the beam, changing the completion can change `G_F` while leaving all
beam evolution unchanged.

The [completion audit](2026-09-15-commutator-completion.md) expresses the
possible finite fields as an exact affine-space family `c + im(A)` under its
declared contracts. Native descendant G is therefore **generally
completion-dependent**, not universally ambiguous at every cell or for every
root. A fixed completion policy makes it well-defined for that policy; the
beam does not select the policy. Shared completion variables can still force
exact parity relations without yielding a native-G transport theorem.

### 4. The historical P/D centered-G carrier

The 2026-09-14 binary-lift census searched a specific period-four/five P/D
grammar with source-dependent recipe selection. Its cumulative first-floor
family has faithful and centered-G carriers for all 256 ECAs, while original
G is carried for 156. The selected repeat carried recursive G for 200 of 220
eligible selected 3D paths; retained alternative recipes raised best-known 3D
coverage to 206. A separate fixed 4D sample had 113 paths, 105 of them
G-passing. No induction theorem was proved for this grammar, and compatibility
can depend on the chosen parent completion.

That narrow recursive native-carrier question remains mathematically
unfinished. It cannot be used to deny the later two-beam ancestral theorem,
and there is no current reason to resume its census.

### 5. The one-beam typed Groovy bundle

Two marked point beams suffice for completion-independent ancestral transport.
One native cellwise G does not. The remaining construction question asks for
the smallest useful object between them: one marked point/frame beam plus a
locally defined tangent or point-vector sector. This typed compression is
open. It is not the same as finding a preferred ambient completion, and the
smaller named Rule-110 cache does not solve its phase-free recursive contract.

## State changes made by this audit

- Arbitrary binary finite-memory parent lifting and recursion through every
  fixed finite depth are marked answered, on the phase-saturated marked beam.
- Historical knowledge entries now distinguish their finite construction from
  the later theorem instead of advertising induction as globally open.
- “Native G remains completion-dependent” is qualified as a general boundary,
  with its exact forced-domain exceptions preserved.
- The active open phrase is now **one-beam typed Groovy compression**, not the
  ambiguous “recursive G.” It remains dormant without a consumer.

## Limits

This synthesis does not prove minimality of two beams, construct the one-beam
typed sector, select an off-beam completion, optimize arbitrary-phase readout,
generalize beyond binary finite-memory CA on integer lattices, or take an
infinite-dimensional limit. Historical experiment notes keep their original
claims; dated knowledge addenda state how the later theorem changes their
current interpretation.
