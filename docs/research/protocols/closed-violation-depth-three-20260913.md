The identity is not something to test at depth three; it is a tool: at any depth `h`, the all-ring depth set `D^h_ψ^∞`, the least ring at which each deep rule leaves `D^h_ψ(n)`, and the eventual period and eventual membership pattern of `D^h_ψ(n)` in `n` are all decided by strong components, cyclic classes and breadth-first search on the pair graph, with **no matrix power**. At depth three this is exactly the part of a ring certificate the eighteenth unit declared out of reach ("would need 65,536-vertex powers, 128 MB per packed power"). This unit computes it: the all-ring depth-three gap for all eight observations, the first failing ring of every rule outside `D³_ψ^∞`, and `P³_ψ`, using the sixteenth and seventeenth units' 65,536-vertex machinery as it stands. What it does not give — the per-ring sets `D³_ψ(n)` between ring 14 and the (unknown) onset — is stated in Section 4. The structural bets are on what the account predicts: which observations' rings-3-to-14 depth-three gaps overstate the all-ring gap, and the eventual periods.

## 1. Objects and definitions

Observations `ψ ∈ {232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 102, 90, 150}`, as in units thirteen to eighteen; rules `r ∈ 0..255`. `h_* ≤ h` at ring `n` means `P_{h+1} = P_h` (`h + 1` steps of observed history determine the next); `D^h_ψ(n) = {r : h_*(r, ψ, n) ≤ h}`; `D^h_ψ^∞ = ⋂_{n ≥ 4} D^h_ψ(n)`, the fourteenth unit's convention (ring 3 is exhaustive-only and reported alongside). `FS^h_ψ` is the full-shift depth-`h` set. Historical sets are **read with their hashes, not recomputed**: `D¹_ψ(n)` for rings 4 to `N¹_ψ + P¹_ψ − 1`, `D¹_ψ^∞`, `FS¹_ψ` and the depth-one `(k, p)` from `results/depth_one_certificate_20260911.json`; `D²_ψ(n)`, `D²_ψ^∞`, `P²_ψ`, `N²_ψ`, the depth-two `(k, p)` and per-rule `|V²_{cl}|` from `results/depth_two_certificate_20260912.json`; `FS²_ψ` from `results/full_shift_depth_two_20260911.json`; `D³_ψ(n)` for `3 ≤ n ≤ 14`, `FS³_ψ` and the rings-3-to-14 depth-three gaps `Γ³_{ψ,[3,14]}` from `results/full_shift_depth_three_20260912.json` (232, 200, 22, 4; 32 by that unit's deduction `F_32 = F_4 ∘ ¬`) and `results/full_shift_depth_three_linear_20260912.json` (90, 150, 165). `FS³_102 = D³_102(n) = ` all 256 rules by the fifteenth unit's M4 (`FS²_102` is everything).

**The depth-`h` pair graph `G^h_{ψ,r}`** is the sixteenth unit's `pair_graph(psi, r, h)`, verbatim: vertices the `4^{2h+2}` pair blocks of `2h + 2` cells; an edge from a `(2h+3)`-cell pair block's first `2h + 2` cells to its last exactly when its centre agrees on `ψ, ψ∘F_r, …, ψ∘F_r^h`; a **violating walk** is a three-edge walk reading a `(2h+5)`-cell pair block whose three sub-blocks are edges and whose `(h+1)`-st observed successors disagree at the centre; `V^h_{ψ,r}` is the set of its end pairs `(v_0, v_3)`. At `h = 1, 2, 3` this is the fourteenth, fifteenth and sixteenth units' graph (256, 4,096, 65,536 vertices). `A` is the adjacency matrix; every vertex has at most four out-edges.

**Ring criterion (theorem, thirteenth/fourteenth/eighteenth units).** For `n ≥ 4`: `r ∉ D^h_ψ(n)` iff some `(v_0, v_3) ∈ V^h_{ψ,r}` has `(A^{n−3})[v_3, v_0] = 1`. **Closed violations.** `V^h_{cl} ⊆ V^h` is the set of violating walks whose ends lie in one strong component of `G^h_{ψ,r}`. The eighteenth unit's pruning lemma: `V^h_{cl} = ∅ ⟹ r ∈ D^h_ψ^∞`.

**Lemma A (the converse; new here, pen).** `V^h_{cl} ≠ ∅ ⟹ r ∉ D^h_ψ^∞`. Proof: take `(v_0, v_3) ∈ V^h_{cl}`. If `v_0 ≠ v_3`, the strong component gives a walk `v_3 ⇝ v_0` of some length `m ≥ 1`. If `v_0 = v_3`, the violating walk itself is a closed walk of length 3 from `v_3` to `v_0`, so `m = 3` works. Either way `(A^m)[v_3, v_0] = 1` with `m ≥ 1`, so `r ∉ D^h_ψ(m + 3)` by the criterion, and `m + 3 ≥ 4`. ∎ Hence, at every depth and for every observation, **`D^h_ψ^∞ = {r : V^h_{cl} = ∅}`** (Corollary A), and the eighteenth unit's post hoc identity at depth two is an instance. Gate 1 is asked to check this proof; if it fails, the unit's structure changes and the protocol is re-frozen.

**First failing ring.** For `r` with `V^h_{cl} ≠ ∅` define `m_min(r) = min` over `(v_0, v_3) ∈ V^h_{cl}` of the least `m ≥ 1` with `(A^m)[v_3, v_0] = 1` — the breadth-first distance from `v_3` to `v_0` if they differ, else the girth through `v_3` (at most 3) — and `n_min(r) = m_min(r) + 3`. Theorem (from the criterion): `n_min(r)` is the least `n ≥ 4` with `r ∉ D^h_ψ(n)`; for `4 ≤ n < n_min(r)`, `r ∈ D^h_ψ(n)`. Only violating walks with both ends in a common strong component can contribute, so the search is over `V^h_{cl}` only.

**Eventual pattern (theorem, standard: Lind–Marcus, *Symbolic Dynamics and Coding*, §4.5; Brualdi–Ryser, *Combinatorial Matrix Theory*, §3.4).** A strong component `C` with at least one edge has a period `p_C` (the gcd of its cycle lengths, computed as the gcd of `level(u) + 1 − level(w)` over edges `u → w` inside `C` from any breadth-first level function) and cyclic classes `c : C → Z/p_C` with `c(w) = c(u) + 1` along every edge. For `u, v ∈ C` there is `M` such that for all `m ≥ M`, `(A^m)[u, v] = 1` iff `m ≡ c(v) − c(u) (mod p_C)`. So `b_m = [∃ (v_0, v_3) ∈ V^h_{cl} : (A^m)[v_3, v_0] = 1]` is eventually the indicator of the residue set `R_r = ⋃_{(v_0,v_3) ∈ V^h_{cl}} { m : m ≡ c(v_0) − c(v_3) (mod p_{C(v_0)}) }`, and `r ∈ D^h_ψ(n)` for all large `n` iff `n − 3 ∉ R_r`. The **eventual period** `p_r` of `r`'s membership is the least period of the indicator of `R_r` (a divisor of `lcm` of the periods involved); `r` is **eventually out** iff `R_r` is everything (`p_r = 1` and `b` eventually 1), **eventually oscillating** otherwise. Corollary B: a closed violating walk lying in a strong component of period 1 makes `r` eventually out; an eventually oscillating rule has all its closed violating walks in components of period `≥ 2`, hence in none containing a self-loop — in particular in none containing a constant diagonal vertex `(x, x)` with `x` constant. The observation-level eventual period is `P^h_ψ = lcm_r p_r`, and the **eventual sets** `D^h_{ψ,ev}(j) = {r : (j − 3) mod P^h_ψ ∉ R_r}` for `j ∈ Z/P^h_ψ` are the sets `D^h_ψ(n)` takes for all `n` beyond some onset. **Nothing here determines the onset**; the eighteenth unit's `(k, p)` does, and at depth three no such certificate is computed (Section 4).

**Conjugates and mirrors.** Complement conjugation `r ↦ r̃`, `ψ ↦ ψ̃` (`232, 150` self-dual; `4 ↔ 223`, `32 ↔ 251`, `200 ↔ 236`, `22 ↔ 151`, `102 ↔ 153`, `90 ↔ 165`) and reflection act on the pair graph as vertex relabelings (reflection reverses walks, transposing `A`); strong components, their periods, `|V^h_{cl}|`, `n_min` and `R_r` (with residues negated under reflection, which does not change `p_r` or membership) are transported. The eighteenth unit verified this graph correspondence at depth two for all 2,048 pairs; here it is a control at depth three on the pairs computed.

(b) Depth two, recomputed: the same against the eighteenth unit's `D²_ψ^∞`, and `|V²_{cl}|` per rule equals its recorded `closed_violating_walks` count. (c) Depth two, first failing ring: for every rule with `V²_{cl} ≠ ∅`, `n_min(r)` from breadth-first search equals the least `n ≥ 4` with `r ∉ D²_ψ(n)` in the eighteenth unit's certified lists (2,048 pairs; the twelve ring-24 rules under 22 must give `m_min = 21`). (d) Depth two, eventual period: for every rule with `V²_{cl} ≠ ∅`, `p_r` from strong-component periods and cyclic classes equals the least period of the eighteenth unit's bit sequence `b` from its `k` (its recorded per-rule `(k, p)` and certified sets suffice to compute this); hence `P²_ψ` = 1, 2, 2, 1, 12, 1, 4, 6 is reproduced and the eventual sets `D²_{ψ,ev}(j)` equal the certified `D²_ψ(n)` on the period from `N²_ψ`. (e) Depth three: every rule in `FS³_ψ` has `V³_{cl} = ∅` (theorem) — checked on a frozen control sample, the eight smallest rule numbers in `FS³_ψ` per observation, since Section 3 does not compute the full-shift rules. A failure of any part of Q1 is an implementation error or a flaw in Section 1 and stops the unit.
- **Q2 (the all-ring depth-three set and gap; the unit's main bets).** For each of the eight observations, `D³_ψ^∞ = FS³_ψ ∪ {r ∉ FS³_ψ : V³_{cl} = ∅}` and `Γ³_{ψ,∞} = D³_ψ^∞ \ FS³_ψ` are computed; `Γ³_{ψ,∞} ⊆ Γ³_{ψ,[3,14]}` (theorem; sizes 8, 10, 10, 11, 8, 0, 4, 0 under 232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 102, 90, 150). Theorem controls: `Γ³_{150,∞} = Γ³_{102,∞} = ∅`. **Bet (a):** `Γ³_{ψ,∞} ≠ ∅` under each of 232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 90 — the all-ring depth-three gap is nonempty for exactly six observations. Interpretation of a nonempty gap: it contains rules that have depth at most three on **every** finite periodic ring `n ≥ 4` (each ring's membership is decided, here and in the sixteenth/seventeenth units) yet have full-shift depth at least four — so even the collection of all periodic-ring tests, taken together, does not decide the full-shift depth-three property under those six observations. It does not mean that any individual ring's depth-three membership is undecided. **Bet (b):** the rings-3-to-14 window overstates the all-ring gap somewhere: `Γ³_{ψ,∞} ≠ Γ³_{ψ,[3,14]}` for at least one of 232, 4, 32, 200, 22, 90, and **(c)** observation 22 is among the failures (the depth-two precedent: the only observation whose rings-3-to-14 gap overstated the all-ring gap, by twelve rules leaving at ring 24). A failure of (b) means every depth-three gap rule keeps depth at most three at every ring, i.e. `n_min > 14` never happens at depth three and the window is exact; (c) fails if all eight of 22's gap rules 140, 141, 162, 163, 176, 177, 196, 197 have `V³_{cl} = ∅`. Each failure of equality is reported with the rule, `n_min`, and the closed violating walk `(v_0, v_3)` with its component's size and period.
- **Q3 (first failing rings at depth three; reported, with a consistency control).** For every `r ∉ D³_ψ^∞` (all rules outside `FS³_ψ` with `V³_{cl} ≠ ∅`), `n_min(r)`. Control against the recorded rings: if `n_min(r) ≤ 14` then `r ∉ D³_ψ(n_min)` and `r ∈ D³_ψ(n)` for `4 ≤ n < n_min`; if `n_min(r) > 14` then `r ∈ D³_ψ(n)` for all `4 ≤ n ≤ 14` (so `r ∈ Γ³_{ψ,[3,14]} \ Γ³_{ψ,∞}` exactly when `n_min > 14`, unless `r ∉ D³_ψ(3)`). Reported without a bet: the distribution of `n_min` per observation, its maximum, and whether any depth-three `n_min` exceeds the depth-two record of 24.
- **Q4 (eventual periods at depth three; bets and kernel controls).** `p_r` for every `r ∉ D³_ψ^∞`, `P³_ψ`, and the eventual sets `D³_{ψ,ev}(j)`. Theorem controls: `D³_{90,ev}(j) = 256` for odd `j` and `D³_{150,ev}(j) = 256` for `3 ∤ j` (kernel facts: 90 has parity's fibres on odd rings, 150 is bijective off multiples of 3, so no ring of those sizes has a closed violation realized — equivalently `R_r` avoids those residues), and `P³_150` is a multiple of 3 (the seventeenth unit's pen-decided rules have `h_* = 4` at rings 6, 9, 12, so `D³_150(n) ≠ 256` on infinitely many multiples of 3). **Bets:** `P³_232 = P³_200 = 1`; `P³_4 = P³_32 = 1` (the depth-two period 2 came from rule 50 alone, and `50 ∈ FS³_4`, so it is trivially in at depth three; the bet is that no other rule oscillates); `3 | P³_22`; `P³_90 = 4` and `P³_150 = 6` (the depth-one and depth-two values under both linear observations). A failure reports the actual period and the oscillating rules with their components' periods.
- **Q5 (structure of the closed violations; reported, one bet).** For every `(ψ, r)` computed at depth three: the number of strong components carrying a closed violating walk, their sizes and periods, and whether any of them contains a diagonal vertex `(x, x)` (a **diagonal-anchored** closed violation: the ring witness pair coincides on a stretch of `2h + 2 = 8` cells), and separately whether any of them contains a **constant** diagonal vertex `(x, x)` with `x` constant (a **constant-diagonal-anchored** closed violation; such a vertex carries a self-loop, since the constant word extends to itself). Theorem control (Corollary B, exactly what it proves): an eventually oscillating rule is never **constant-diagonal**-anchored — its closed violating components have period `≥ 2`, so none contains a self-loop. Checked at depth two on the recorded oscillating rules 50 under 4, 179 under 32, and the 33, 14, 22 under 22, 90, 150 — read from the eighteenth unit's certified sets — and at depth three on whatever oscillates. Corollary B says **nothing** about general (nonconstant) diagonal vertices: a nonconstant `(x, x)` need not carry a self-loop and can lie in a component of period `≥ 2`, so a general diagonal anchor in an eventually oscillating rule is a reported observation, not a violation, and the implementation must not treat it as an error. General diagonal anchoring of oscillating rules is reported without a bet. **Bet:** every eventually-out rule at depth three (`p_r = 1`, `b` eventually 1) under 232, 200 and 22 has a diagonal-anchored closed violation — its depth-three failure can be witnessed by a ring pair that agrees on eight consecutive cells, a localized hidden difference. No bet under 4, 32, 90, 150 (no prior; rule 4's observation is blind inside blocks of ones in a way that may force global witnesses). A failure lists the eventually-out rules whose every closed violating component avoids the diagonal.
- **Q6 (symmetries; theorem controls).** For every computed pair, the conjugate pair `(ψ̃, r̃)` (computed directly for 223, 251, 236, 151, 165; 232 and 150 self-dual, checked as `r ↦ r̃` invariance) and, for the asymmetric observations, the mirror pair have the same `|V³_{cl}|`, `n_min`, `p_r`, `R_r` up to the sign convention, component sizes and periods, and diagonal anchoring, under the explicit vertex maps. At the set level: `D³_{ψ̃}^∞ = {r̃ : r ∈ D³_ψ^∞}`, `Γ³` and `P³` transported. In particular `D³_32^∞`, computed directly here, must equal the conjugate transport of `D³_4^∞` — the first direct check of the sixteenth unit's deduction for 32 at the all-ring level.
