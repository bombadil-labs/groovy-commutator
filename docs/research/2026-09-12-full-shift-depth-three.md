## The question

The fifteenth unit left 24 pairs `(ψ, r)` under 232, 200 and 22 whose rules have refinement depth at most one at every ring and at least three on the full shift. This unit asks how deep they are, and tabulates every rule's full-shift depth class against its rings-3-to-14 class, under the four non-linear observations that carry gaps: 232, 200, 22 and 4, with their complement conjugates 236, 151 and 223 computed directly, and observation 32 obtained by the recorded deduction (`F_32 = F_4∘¬`, `F_223 = ¬∘F_32`, output complement preserves equality fibers, so every set under 32 is the conjugate transport of the set under 4).

The depth-three pair graph `G³_{ψ,r}` has the 65,536 eight-cell pair blocks as vertices, an edge per nine-cell pair block whose centre agrees on `ψ`, `ψ∘F_r`, `ψ∘F_r²`, `ψ∘F_r³`, and a violating three-edge walk per 11-cell pair block whose fifth observed successors disagree at the centre, 4,194,304 blocks per pair. `r ∈ FS³_ψ` iff no violating walk is bi-infinitely extendable, decided by strong components and two breadth-first searches on the sparse edge list. No matrix power is taken and no ring certificate at depth two or three is claimed. The full-shift depth class `c_FS ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, ≥4}` of a rule is read from the thirteenth to fifteenth units' closed and depth sets and `FS³`; the rings-3-to-14 class `c_R` from the intersections of the exhaustive sets over rings 3 to 14.

## Answer

- **N1, theorem controls.** `FS²_ψ ⊆ FS³_ψ`, `FS³_ψ ⊆ D³_ψ(n)`, `D²_ψ(n) ⊆ D³_ψ(n)`, divisibility `D³_ψ(kn) ⊆ D³_ψ(n)` and `c_FS ≥ c_R` for every rule all held, for the four observations and their conjugates. `FS³` has 198, 181, 234 and 106 rules under 232, 200, 22 and 4, against `FS²` of 178, 172, 186 and 97.
- **N2, the deep rules: the bet held, and more strongly than bet.** Of the 24 pairs, only 4 have full-shift depth exactly three, all under 22 (rules 143, 166, 180, 213). The other 20 have full-shift depth at least four: all eight under 232 (58, 78, 92, 114, 141, 163, 177, 197), all four under 200 (135, 149, 157, 199) and eight under 22 (140, 141, 162, 163, 176, 177, 196, 197). Each carries an extendable violating walk in `G³_{ψ,r}` with its reachability record; rule 58 under 232, for instance, has 848 violating walks of which 12 are extendable, its start lying in a 256-vertex strong component. So a rule can have depth at most one on every ring and depth at least four on the full shift, and this is the common case among the deep rules, not the exception.
- **N3, the depth-three gap: the bet held.** The all-tested-ring gap `Γ³_{ψ,[3,14]} = (⋂_{3≤n≤14} D³_ψ(n)) \ FS³_ψ` is nonempty under each of the four: 8 rules under 232 (the eight deep rules), 11 under 200 (41, 97, 135, 137, 149, 157, 169, 193, 199, 225, 233), 8 under 22 (the eight deep rules) and 10 under 4 (9, 25, 41, 45, 65, 67, 79, 93, 97, 101). The ring-14 gap `Γ³_{ψ,14}` has 10, 13, 8 and 10 rules; under 232 and 200 it adds rules that leave `D³` on some smaller ring. The depth-three sets on rings 3 to 14 still vary with the ring under every observation (under 4 from 256 at ring 3 to 116 at ring 14), so neither gap is an all-ring statement.
- **N4, the ladder: the bet held.** Class sizes `c_FS = 0, 1, 2, 3, ≥4`: 232 (22, 80, 76, 20, 58); 200 (42, 94, 36, 9, 75); 22 (11, 82, 93, 48, 22); 4 (33, 48, 16, 9, 150). Class sizes `c_R`: 232 (22, 96, 80, 8, 50); 200 (42, 102, 40, 8, 64); 22 (11, 104, 101, 26, 14); 4 (33, 48, 20, 15, 140). The class `c_FS = 3` is nonempty under every observation (20, 9, 48, 9 rules). The cross-tabulation is diagonal at classes 0 and 1 except for the fourteenth unit's gap rules, and off-diagonal above: under 232 eight rules sit at `(c_FS, c_R) = (2, 1)`, twelve at `(3, 2)` and eight at `(≥4, 1)`; under 200 the off-diagonal cells are `(2,1)` 4, `(3,2)` 6, `(≥4,1)` 4, `(≥4,2)` 2, `(≥4,3)` 5; under 22 `(2,1)` 10, `(3,1)` 4, `(3,2)` 18, `(≥4,1)` 8; under 4 `(≥4,2)` 4 and `(≥4,3)` 6, with no rule at `(2,1)` or `(3,2)`. Closure (class 0) is the only level where the two ladders coincide exactly, as the thirteenth unit certified; at every depth level from one upward some rules sit strictly higher on the full shift than on the rings.
- **N5, explicit witness pairs (control on the verifier).** All 24 depth-two separations were realized as eventually periodic pairs by breadth-first search in `G²_{ψ,r}`; tail periods 1, 2, 3 or 6, longest window 48 cells; on every window `ψ`, `ψ∘F_r` and `ψ∘F_r²` agree on the interior trimmed by four cells and `ψ∘F_r³` differs at the centre. The graph path is the certificate; the replay passed for all 24.
- **N6, symmetries (theorem controls).** `FS³` and every `D³_ψ(n)` computed directly for 236, 151 and 223 are the conjugate transports of the sets for 200, 22 and 4 (232 is self-dual), and all four observations are reflection-symmetric with reflection-invariant sets.

## Reading for the program

The sparse method now reaches three levels of the full-shift ladder at a cost of one reachability problem on `4^{2h+2}` vertices per pair, and at each level it separates the full shift from the periodic configurations. The separation is not a thin boundary effect: twenty rules under three observations need at most one step of observed history on every finite ring and at least four on the line, and under 232 and 200 every rule that the fifteenth unit found beyond depth two is beyond depth three as well. The ladder table is the concise statement of the program's finding about depth: closure is decided by periodic configurations, depth is not, and the discrepancy grows with the level rather than closing.

## Limits

Four observations and their conjugates, exhaustive rings 3 to 14, full-shift depth at most three only; rules in the top class have full-shift depth at least four and nothing more is certified about them. No ring certificate at depth two or three, so every gap is a rings-3-to-14 statement. Observation 32 is a recorded deduction, not a computation; nothing about 102, 90, 150 or other observations, or about rings 1 and 2. No Class IV, novelty, renormalization or dimension claim.
