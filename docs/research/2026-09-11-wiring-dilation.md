## The transformation

The dilated automaton reads the same 8-entry rule table at offsets `{−d, 0, +d}` instead of `{−1, 0, +1}`; the dilated observation is defined the same way. Two transports were declared: whole-contract dilation (rule and observation together) and partial dilation (rule only). Domain: `d ∈ {2, 3}`, rings 6 to 12, observations 232 (majority) and 4 (isolated cell), with the eighth and ninth units' censuses as references, plus the result-1 commutator classification of the realized state map. The unit was prompted by the interaction-graph framing in B. Rose, *Cellular Automata as Interaction Graphs* (CargoCultResearch preprint, 2026), which treats the wiring as the type and the rule table as the invariant.

## Answer

- **Reference control.** The standard census recomputed here equals the eighth and ninth units' result files at every ring for closed sets, commuters and depths.
- **W1, whole-contract dilation is a relabeling (theorem control).** For every `(d, n)` with `gcd(d, n) = 1`, 14 cells in all, the census of the dilated rules under the dilated observation, `C`, `K`, `Z`, `X` and `h_*` for all 256 rules, is identical to the standard census at that ring, and so is the commutator classification of every realized map. The site permutation `i ↦ d·i mod n` conjugates one automaton to the other, so nothing can differ, and nothing did.
- **W2, partial dilation is a change.** For `d = 2` at rings 7, 9 and 11 and both observations: (a) the closed set of the dilated rules under the standard observation differs from the standard closed set, and (b) the observation rule itself leaves the commuter set, while 0, 170, 204 and 240 stay. Both held at every cell, scored separately. The consistency control held everywhere: the partial census equals the standard census under the observation rewired to offsets `{−e, 0, +e}` with `e = 2^{−1} mod n` (`e = 4, 5, 6` at rings 7, 9, 11). Reported, post hoc: the partial closed sets are the same at all three odd rings. Under majority the closed set shrinks from 22 to 12 rules, `{0, 4, 15, 32, 51, 85, 170, 204, 223, 240, 251, 255}`, with commuters `{0, 15, 51, 85, 170, 204, 240, 255}` and a residual class `{4, 32, 223, 251}`; rules 232 and 23 are no longer commuters. Under isolated-cell detection the closed set shrinks from 33 to 17 rules, `{0, 170, 187, 189, 191, 204, 207, 221, 223, 231, 239, 240, 243, 247, 251, 253, 255}`, with commuters reduced to `{0, 170, 204, 240}` and an empty residual class. The depth tables differ from the standard ones at every cell; the maximum depth falls from 8 to 6 under majority at ring 11 and from 6, 14, 12 to 5, 6, 9 under isolated-cell detection at rings 7, 9, 11.
- **W3, even rings split (theorem control).** For `d = 2` at rings 6, 8, 10 and 12 under whole-contract dilation, the census equals the standard census on the ring of half the size for `C`, `K`, `Z`, `X` and `h_*`, at every cell and for both observations, and so does the commutator classification. The small-ring censuses, outside every earlier unit's domain, are reported: under majority the closed set has 256, 64, 64, 64 and 22 rules on rings 2 to 6, with maximum depth 0, 2, 2, 2, 2; under isolated-cell detection 256, 160, 104, 37 and 33 rules, with maximum depth 0, 1, 2, 4, 4.
- **W4, dilation by 3 (theorem control).** At rings 7, 8, 10 and 11 the whole-contract census equals the standard census, as in W1. At rings 6, 9 and 12 the automaton splits into three rings of size 2, 3 and 4, and the census equals the small-ring census, depth and commutator classification included.
- **W5, the commutator classification (reported).** At every cell the classification under partial dilation equals that under whole-contract dilation, since both use the same realized map. At every coprime cell it equals the standard classification: 10 zero-`G` rules, 8 one-`G` rules, 238 varying, the result-1 sets. On split rings it equals the classification of the component ring, which departs from the result-1 sets when the component ring has fewer than 5 cells, where the five-cell window that decides the classification does not fit: 30, 22, 204 on a component ring of 3; 12, 10, 234 on 4; 96, 96, 64 on 2; and the standard 10, 8, 238 on 5 and 6.

## Reading for the program

Wiring is now a fourth transformation type in the program's vocabulary, and dilation sorts cleanly into the three verdicts the program uses. With the observation transported alongside the rule and a factor coprime to the ring, every audited property is preserved exactly, because the transformation is a site relabeling in disguise; this is the wiring-side twin of the reflection result from the first unit. With the observation left standing, the same rewiring is a real change: the commuter set loses the observation rule, the closed set shrinks, and the depth table moves, and the change is exactly the change of observing the standard automaton through an inverse-offset rewiring of the observation. When the factor divides the ring, the system is several smaller systems interleaved, and every census says so, including the commutator classification's departure from the result-1 sets on components too small to hold a five-cell window. For a learner in the planned primitives program, the moral is the same as for complement conjugation: a change of wiring is a coordinate change only when the whole contract travels with it.

## Limits

Dilation of the three-cell neighborhood on a ring, factors 2 and 3, rings 6 to 12, two observations. Nothing about other offset sets, 2D lattices, or block and second-order constructions. The partial-dilation censuses are exact within the domain and characterize nothing beyond it; that their closed sets coincide across the three odd rings is a census fact. Small-ring figures are reported only. No Class IV, novelty, or renormalization claim.
