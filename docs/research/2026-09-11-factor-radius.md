## The question

For a rule `r` closed under an observation `ψ`, the factor `B_r` is the map on the image with `B_r(ψ(S)) = ψ(F_r S)`. Its locality radius on the image, `ρ_r(n)`, is the least `ρ ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}` for which the `(2ρ+1)`-window table read off image words at ring `n` has no conflicting entry, with unseen windows as don't-cares. The [tenth unit](2026-09-11-complement-observation.md) had reported sixteen closed rules under observation 200 whose factor is not radius-1 consistent, without explanation. The organizing property is idempotence of the observation: 200 and 4 satisfy `ψ ∘ ψ = ψ` at every ring; 232, 32 and 22 do not, and the verifier confirms both facts on every ring 6 to 12.

## Answer

- **Reference control.** The closed sets recomputed here equal the eighth, ninth and tenth units' result files at every ring for all five observations.
- **R1, idempotent observations (theorem control).** Under 200 and 4 the image is the fixed-point set, so every image word is its own preimage and the factor of every closed rule equals `ψ ∘ F_r` restricted to the image; the verifier confirms the equality for every closed rule at every ring. Hence `ρ ≤ 2`, and it is.
- **R2, the sixteen need exactly radius 2.** Under 200 the sixteen residual rules `{72, 76, 128, 130, 132, 136, 140, 144, 152, 160, 162, 176, 183, 192, 194, 196}` have `ρ = 2` at every ring 6 to 12, and the other 26 closed rules have `ρ ≤ 1`: 16 at radius 0 and 10 at radius 1 (`{42, 55, 63, 112, 119, 127, 170, 191, 240, 247}`). Under 4 all 33 closed rules have `ρ ≤ 1`: 26 at radius 0, 7 at radius 1. So the non-elementary factors reported by the tenth unit are exactly the closed rules that use the full radius that `ψ ∘ F_r` has by construction, and no closed rule needs more.
- **R3, non-idempotent observations: failed at one cell.** Under 232 every closed rule has `ρ ≤ 1` at every ring (12 at radius 0, 10 at radius 1); under 32 likewise (26 and 7); under 22 at rings 7 to 12 likewise (7 and 4). At ring 6 under 22 the closed set has one extra member, rule 223, the ring-dependent closure reported by the tenth unit, and its factor has `ρ = 3`, a seven-cell window on a six-cell ring, which is the whole configuration; at radius 2 the single window `00100` conflicts. So the one failure is a closed rule whose closure is itself a finite-ring fact, and its factor has no consistent window smaller than the whole six-cell configuration. Every other cell in the non-idempotent census is at radius 1 or less, stronger than the frozen bound of 2, as a census fact.
- **R4, collapse and commuters (control).** Every collapse rule has `ρ = 0` and every exact commuter has `ρ ≤ 1`, under every observation and ring. Post hoc: radius 0 is not only collapse; a factor that acts cellwise, such as the identity's under every observation, the complement's under majority, or rule 4's own on its image of isolated ones, is also radius 0.
- **R5, ring independence.** Frozen half held: under 200 and 4 every closed rule's radius is the same at rings 7 to 12 (and, as reported, at ring 6 too). Reported: under 232, 32 and 22 every rule closed at all seven rings has the same radius at all seven; the only ring-dependent cell in the census is rule 223 at ring 6 under 22, which is closed there only.
- **R6, symmetries.** Radius tables are reflection-invariant for all five observations, and complement-covariant between 200 and 236 and between 4 and 223, the conjugate-observation controls.

## Reading for the program

The tenth unit's puzzle has a one-line answer: under an idempotent observation the factor of a closed rule is the observation composed with the rule, so it has radius 2 whenever the rule genuinely uses both neighbors of a neighbor, and the sixteen rule-200 cases are exactly those. Under the non-idempotent observations the factor never needed more than radius 1 anywhere the closure was ring-independent, though nothing proves it must not, and the single radius-3 cell shows what one finite-ring closure looks like from the inside: the "factor" exists only because the whole configuration fits in one window. In this census the sole ring-dependent closure is also the sole factor requiring whole-ring context, so whole-ring support is a useful warning sign here, not a proved characterization of finite-ring closures in general. For the primitives program, whole-ring factor support is worth checking when a closure appears at one ring only.

## Limits

Five observations on rings 6 to 12, radii up to 3, closed rules only. The idempotence argument is a theorem for its two observations; every other radius is a census fact, including the radius-1 uniformity under 232, 32 and 22 and the ring-6 exception. Nothing is claimed about the factor off the image, about other observations, or about idempotence characterizing bounded radius in general. No Class IV, novelty, or renormalization claim.
