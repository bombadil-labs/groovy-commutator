## The question

The [eighteenth unit](2026-09-12-depth-two-certificate.md) ended with an unpredicted exact observation: under all eight audited observations, the all-ring depth-two set `D²_ψ^∞` equalled precisely the set of rules whose depth-two pair graph has no violating three-edge walk with both ends inside one strong component. It called that "the most suggestive thing in the run and the least established," a candidate object for a structural account of the deep rules.

On inspection the identity is not a coincidence of eight graphs but a theorem with a three-line proof, at every depth. The eighteenth unit already had the easy direction (its pruning lemma: no closed violating walk implies all-ring depth). The converse — the protocol's **Lemma A** — is just as short: if a violating pair `(v_0, v_3)` lies in one strong component, the component supplies a return walk `v_3 ⇝ v_0` of some length `m ≥ 1` (and when the ends coincide, the violating walk is itself a closed walk of length 3), so the fourteenth unit's ring criterion fails at ring `m + 3 ≥ 4`. Hence at every depth and for every observation,

`D^h_ψ^∞ = {r : V^h_cl = ∅}` (Corollary A).

That changes what the object is for. It is not a conjecture to re-test at depth three; it is a decision procedure. Together with the standard eventual-pattern theorem for irreducible components (Lind–Marcus §4.5; Brualdi–Ryser §3.4), strong components, cyclic classes and breadth-first search decide, at depth three, three things the eighteenth unit had declared out of reach — it would have needed "65,536-vertex powers at 128 MB each": the all-ring depth-three set `D³_ψ^∞` and its gap against the full shift, each deep rule's **first failing ring** `n_min`, and the **eventual period** `P³_ψ` and eventual sets of `D³_ψ(n)` in the ring size. **No power of any matrix is taken at any depth.** What the method does not give is the *onset* beyond which the eventual pattern starts; that is stated as not claimed, and the per-ring sets between ring 14 and the unknown onset remain undecided.

The questions, then: is the all-ring depth-three gap nonempty, and where does the sixteenth and seventeenth units' rings-3-to-14 window overstate it; how late can a rule's first depth-three failure be; what are the eventual periods at depth three; and do the depth-two periods carry up a level.

Nothing about full-shift depth is recomputed. `FS¹`, `FS²`, `FS³`, the rings-3-to-14 sets and gaps, the depth-one and depth-two certificates are read from the fourteenth, fifteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth and eighteenth units' result files with their hashes, and the pair graph itself is imported from the sixteenth unit's script rather than restated.

## Reading for the program

The eighteenth unit left an identity it could not explain and could not use. Turning it into Lemma A converts a census problem into a graph problem and buys a whole depth level: the all-ring depth-three set, which that unit declared out of reach on cost grounds, is computed for every rule under every one of these observations in 71 minutes with no matrix power at all. The lesson for the program's method is specific — when a ring-indexed property is decided by a return walk, the expensive object is the power sequence and the cheap object is the strong-component structure, and the cheap one answers "at every ring" questions directly while the expensive one is only needed for the onset. The onset is exactly what is still missing here, and the program should not pretend otherwise: between ring 14 and an undetermined onset, the per-ring depth-three sets remain unknown.

What the failed bets say is that depth levels do not inherit each other's arithmetic. Going into this run, the natural expectation was that the depth-two picture would lift: 22 was the observation whose ring window lied, so it would lie again; the periods 12, 4, 6 and 2 would persist or grow. Neither happened. The window that overstates at depth three belongs to **200**, and 22's window is exact; the period under 22 collapses from 12 to 1 while the period under 4 stays at 2 on a different set of rules. The seventeenth unit had already found that whether periodic configurations decide a level is a property of the pair (observation, level) rather than of depth; this unit extends that to the *quantitative* invariants — the first failing ring and the eventual period are properties of (observation, level) too, and a depth-`h` number is not evidence about depth `h + 1` even under the same observation. A verdict matrix for this program should therefore carry the level with every ring-window and period claim, and should not interpolate.

The structural finding under 200 is the closest thing here to a candidate account of *why* particular rules are deep, and it is narrow. Among 200's eventually-out rules, the ones that survive the longest rings are exactly the ones whose single carrying component contains no diagonal vertex: their failure cannot be witnessed by a pair that agrees on an eight-cell block, which suggests — not establishes — that such a witness needs more room. That is a mechanism of the right shape for the "structural account of the deep rules" the checkpoint asked for — it relates lateness to a geometric property of the witness rather than to the rule table — but it is one observation, post hoc, a coincidence rather than a proven causal account, and the same correlation does not arise under 232, where the two non-anchored rules fail early.

Nothing about depth four or beyond on any ring or on the full shift: a rule outside `D³_ψ^∞` has `h_* ≥ 4` at ring `n_min` and nothing more, and `FS³_ψ` is read with its hash, not recomputed, so full-shift depth four stays parked pending Myk's cost decision. Corollary A is a statement about the pair graph's strong components, not an account in terms of rule tables; the Q5 anchoring observation is one structural property of the witnesses, not an explanation of why particular rules are deep, and its correlation with lateness is post hoc on a single observation. Rules inside `FS³_ψ` were not computed at depth three beyond the 56-pair control sample; their `V³_cl = ∅` rests on the theorem and on the sixteenth and seventeenth units' `FS³` with its hash. Nothing about observations outside the eight and their conjugates and mirrors, about rings 1 and 2, or about ring 3, which is reported from the exhaustive sets and is not covered by the criterion. No Class IV, universality, novelty, renormalization, dimension, physical or metaphysical claim.

**Preserved implementation notes — this unit needed three review-driven corrections before any evidence was produced, and none of them is rewritten away.** (i) Sub-PR #219 committed the verifier with no result. Since `(A'^m)[M(v_0), M(v_3)] = (A^m)[v_3, v_0]` for every `m`, the return-length set and hence `R_r` are **equal**, not negated; the two sign changes cancel.
