## The transformation

`π(S)_i = S_i ⊕ S_{i+1}`, elementary rule 102 applied once as an observation at cadence one. It is XOR-linear and two-to-one on every ring: the fibers are exactly the complement pairs `{S, ¬S}`, the image is the even-parity configurations, one bit per ring is lost (the logarithm of the fiber size, a uniform-prior conditional entropy, not a statement for every prior), forward locality is radius 1, and there is no local inverse: a right-inverse fixes an anchor bit and reconstructs by prefix parity. This is overlapping neighbor parity at the original lattice spacing, distinct from the non-overlapping dyadic, cadence-two coarse-graining cited as context in the [history-repairability note](2026-09-07-history-repairability.md). Under a non-injective map "preserved" gives way to "closed": a property survives when the observed dynamics admits a factor `B` with `π ∘ F = B ∘ π`, the closure notion of [Research022](2026-09-08-observation-closure.md) and the [shared closure account](2026-09-10-shared-closure-account.md).

## Answer

- **C1, closure classification.** Factor closure under `π` holds for exactly 32 rules at every ring size tested, `n ∈ {4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12}`: the 16 complement-invariant rules `{0, 24, 36, 60, 66, 90, 102, 126, 129, 153, 165, 189, 195, 219, 231, 255}` and the 16 self-dual rules `{15, 23, 43, 51, 77, 85, 105, 113, 142, 150, 170, 178, 204, 212, 232, 240}`, the rules whose complement response `g_r = r ⊕ r∘¬` is constant. The prediction covered `n ≥ 6`; rings 4 and 5 were reported without prediction and show no finite-size admissions.
- **C2, the factor is elementary.** For each closed rule the factor extracted on the `n = 8` image is a radius-1 rule on the parity field and equals the frozen formula (`B(y)_i = q(y_{i−1}, y_i) ⊕ q(y_i, y_{i+1})`, plus `y_i` for self-dual rules). Rule 90 maps to rule 90. All 16 affine rules are closed and map to linear factors; the 16 nonlinear closed rules map to factors of algebraic degree exactly 2. Post hoc, not predicted: the factor map is exactly two-to-one, pairing each rule with its complement-conjugate (`r` and `r̃` share a factor, which is immediate once seen: `π F_r̃(S) = π ¬F_r(¬S) = π F_r(¬S) = B π(S)`), so the 32 closed rules have 16 factors `{0, 18, 46, 60, 72, 90, 102, 116, 132, 150, 170, 184, 204, 222, 226, 240}`; the fixed points are exactly the 8 linear rules `{0, 60, 90, 102, 150, 170, 204, 240}`.

- **C3, commutator covariance (theorem control).** `π ∘ G_r = G_B ∘ π` on all states of rings 8 and 10 for all 32 closed rules; the run is a control of the intertwining proof. The 8 one-G rules are all closed and their factors are linear rules with `G_B ≡ 0` on every configuration; the 16 nonlinear closed rules have factors whose commutator varies with state. Every factor's result-1 class computed at `n = 8` and `n = 10` agrees with the established zero-G / one-G / varying sets.
- **C4, refinement census for the non-closed rules.** With each complement pair counted once (denominator `2^{n−1}`), the stabilization depth `h_*` of the [Research023](2026-09-08-history-lift-closure.md) refinement chain is `0` exactly on the 32 closed rules at every `n ∈ {6, 8, 10, 12}`, as the shared closure account's identity requires. Reported, not predicted: for the 224 non-closed rules `h_*` is `1` for 216 rules and `2` for 8 rules, `{22, 73, 104, 109, 146, 151, 182, 233}` (four complement-conjugate pairs, each rule mirror-symmetric), at every ring size tested; no rule's `h_*` changes with `n`. The fraction of complement pairs that ever separate is high and rises with `n`: at `n = 12` there are 2,048 pairs and the median non-closed rule separates 2,046 of them (minimum 1,885, maximum 2,046; fractions 0.920 to 0.999, read from the canonical JSON; an earlier version of this sentence wrongly said 1,022 of 1,024, corrected 2026-09-11 after Codex's gate-2 review); no non-closed rule separates every pair, because `{0ⁿ, 1ⁿ}` is a complement pair that stays equal or complementary under every rule. Termination was by the frozen `2^{n+1}`-step bound (equality absorbing) for the rules with unsplit pairs, never by an arbitrary cutoff.
- **C5, relation to the first audit.** The closed set equals the union of the [first audit's](2026-09-11-representation-invariants-audit.md) saved self-dual set (the rules whose native commutator is complement-covariant) and the complement-invariant rules; the saved set matches the local recomputation.

## Reading for the program

Non-injective coarse-graining separates the rule space along the same local object the first audit found under complement conjugation: the complement response. Constant response gives an elementary factor, commutator covariance, and no memory; non-constant response gives a factor that does not exist and a history requirement that is small and, within the tested rings, size-independent. That `h_* ≤ 2` for all 256 rules at `n ≤ 12` is the unit's one surprise and is **post hoc**: a proof or a failure at larger `n` is a candidate for a frozen follow-up, not a result here. For the primitives program: two constructions that differ by `π` have the same decoded dynamics only for the 32 closed rules; for the rest the coarse view is a different system with at most two steps of memory, and the difference is the erased complement distinction, exactly the Erased Distinctions object `R_∞`.

## Limits

One coarse-graining at cadence one; nothing about block majority, 2-block projection, non-linear observations, `k`-cell parities with `k > 2`, or the infinite line beyond the finite identities. `h_* ≤ 2` and the split fractions are exhaustive at `n ≤ 12` only. C3 is a theorem and its run is a control; C5 is a deduction. No Class IV, novelty, or renormalization claim.
